# Two jumps around each camp block

**The ask:** a CMJ on the force plates **immediately before the first practice** of a
camp block and **immediately after the last one**.

Two tests. Roughly five minutes per athlete, twice per block. Nothing new is purchased,
no new staff time beyond setting the plates out, and no change to how practice is run.

---

## What we can already see

The Kinexon sensors gave us a complete picture of the FA26 camp block — six practices
across three days, sixteen athletes, every jump counted.

| Practice | Length | Squad jumps | Jumps/min |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 — Thu am | 3h 10m | 946 | 4.97 |
| 2 — Thu pm | 1h 31m | 529 | 5.83 |
| 3 — Fri am | 2h 40m | **1,780** | **11.13** |
| 4 — Fri pm | 3h 10m | 991 | 5.21 |
| 5 — Sat am | 3h 46m | 943 | 4.17 |
| 6 — Sat pm | 1h 36m | 900 | 9.39 |

Practice 3 produced **more than three times the jumps of Practice 2** and nearly twice
the per-minute density of Practice 5. That is a large, real difference in what the
athletes were asked to absorb, inside a single three-day block.

Practice 1 was exported drill by drill, and inside that one session:

| Drill | Jumps/min |
|---|---:|
| Box Drill | **26.8** |
| Hurricane | 12.4 |
| Correction Exchange | 11.4 |
| Bunch Drill | 6.6 |
| Whirlwind | 3.9 |

A **seven-fold** spread between the heaviest and lightest drill. The session total hides
all of it.

## What we cannot see, and why it matters

We know precisely how much work was done. We know nothing about what it cost.

Every athlete has a force plate baseline from before camp — most from 6 July, three and
a half weeks out. **Not one has a test after the block.** So the question the load data
naturally raises — *did three days of two-a-days actually take anything out of them, and
out of whom* — has no answer.

That is not a limitation of the sensors or the force plates. It is one missing
measurement.

## What two tests would buy

**1. Whether the block cost anything at all.**
A pre-to-post change per athlete. Our current honest position on the football side is
that a normal training week does not measurably fatigue athletes — but we cannot tell
"the week is well designed" apart from "we cannot see what the week is doing." Camp is
different from a normal week, and camp is where any effect should be largest.

**2. Who tolerates it and who does not.**
The average is the least interesting part. Two athletes can absorb the same load and
respond in opposite directions, and the squad mean cancels them out. Pre/post pairs are
what separate those two athletes.

**3. Over several blocks, an individual profile.**
Once an athlete has a handful of paired blocks, we can say something specific about
*her* — that she handles high jump density well but not high total volume, or the
reverse. That is the version of this worth having, and it only accumulates if the pairs
start getting collected.

## Why camp specifically, and not every week

Because camp is where the contrast is. Our football data shows an in-season week barely
varies — week-to-week load has a coefficient of variation of 0.06–0.10, so there is
almost nothing to compare against. This volleyball camp block has a **3.4× spread in
volume between its own practices**. If training dose moves neuromuscular readiness at
all, a camp block is where it will show.

We are not asking for daily testing. We are asking for the two ends of each block.

## One honest caveat

A single force plate test on a single athlete is a noisy number — week-to-week
measurement error on the best CMJ metric runs around 6–7%. We would not act on one
athlete's one-day change, and we will not present it that way.

What pre/post pairs give us is the *pattern*: across sixteen athletes and repeated
blocks, whether the group moves, and whether particular athletes move consistently. That
is a trustworthy signal built out of individually noisy parts, and it is the only route
to the individual profiles above.

## What we do with the results

The practice-load dashboard already has the comparison built and waiting. The day a
post-block test is recorded, it fills in automatically — per athlete, pre to post,
alongside exactly what she absorbed during the block. No analysis backlog, no delay.

## The specific request

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Morning of the first practice of a block, before it starts | CMJ, 3 jumps |
| After the last practice of the block | CMJ, 3 jumps |

If only one is possible, **the post-block test is the one to keep** — we can fall back on
a recent baseline for the "before", but nothing substitutes for the "after".
