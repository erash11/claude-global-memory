# Shareable — safe to paste into email or Teams

Discussion items beyond what the executive summary covers, roughly in order of how
much they need a decision.

**1. Trial and pilot pathway.** The Technology Idea Form asks "Is this a trial or
pilot?" and offers "Provisional / trial" as an approval status, and the Evaluation
Scorecard offers "Approve as trial with hard end date." The framework body does not
currently define a trial: what it is, who tracks the end date, or whether it
re-enters the process to become permanent. Worth deciding whether to add a short
trials section or remove the option from the instruments so the two agree.

**2. Route for zero-cost tools.** The Business Office is the first control point and
filters on budget, contract, and duplication. A free or no-cost application has none
of the three, and Level 1 keys partly on cost. Worth confirming what routes a no-cost
tool into the process, since a free tool touching athlete data is a Level 3 request
with no budget footprint.

**3. Baseline inventory of technology already in use.** The framework establishes a
scheduled renewal decision for requests that come through it. It does not create the
inventory of what the department already owns or when those agreements end. Owner and
timeline for that baseline are not yet assigned.

**4. Student-Athlete Notice.** The term is defined in §2.4 but is not required at any
phase or review level. Given that §3 positions this framework as Baylor's
implementation of NCAA SSI guidance, and that guidance emphasizes athlete
transparency, worth deciding whether notice becomes a required step and at which
level.

**5. Data handling at discontinuation.** Phase 3 can conclude "discontinue," and
Procurement's role requires contracts to address retention, deletion, and
portability. There is no phase step that executes return or deletion of athlete data
when a product is discontinued.

**6. Scope confirmation.** The framework is written to cover all Athletics
technology, with athlete performance technology as the originating context and
highest-risk category. Worth confirming that department-wide scope is what the group
intends.

**7. Effective date and in-flight requests.** No start date is stated, and there is
no transition handling for requests already underway or for purchases made outside
the process.

# Prep notes — do not send

Filter: every item requires someone else to **agree, decide, or commit a resource**.
Descriptive material is already in the summary and was cut.

## 1. Trials and pilots — a verified inconsistency, and the strongest item

**Verified:** Form 1 (`Form_1_Technology_Idea_Form.docx`) asks "Is this a trial or
pilot?" and lists "☐ Provisional / trial" among approval outcomes. The Scorecard
lists "☐ Approve as trial with hard end date." The framework body contains **zero**
occurrences of trial, pilot, or demo.

**Why the mismatch exists:** all three instruments are dated Aug 4. The §8.2
trials/pilots section was cut from the Aug 11 base. The forms were never revised to
match — a loose end from the merge, not a defect.

**Say:** "The forms and scorecard both offer a trial approval, but the framework
doesn't define one. Do we want a short trials paragraph back, or should the trial
option come off the forms?"

**Why it matters beyond tidiness:** a trial is the most common way technology enters
a department, and it's the case where the cost trigger and the data trigger diverge
hardest — a free 30-day trial reads as low-cost while potentially sending athlete
data to a vendor. If a trial can be approved with a hard end date, someone has to own
that date or the trial quietly becomes permanent.

## 2. Zero-cost tools — the structural version of the same problem

The Business Office is the **first** control point, filtering on "budget, contract,
and duplication." A free app has no budget line, no contract, and often no
duplication signal. Meanwhile Level 1 applies when there is "no athlete data; single
unit; low or one-time cost."

So the entry point least likely to trip the filter is the one that can carry the most
risk: a free tool that touches athlete data. Nothing in Phase 1 routes a no-cost
request differently.

**Say:** "What gets a free tool into the funnel? The Business Office filter is budget
and contract, and a free app has neither."

**What you want:** agreement that data category, not cost, is what triggers intake —
and ideally one sentence added to Phase 1 saying so.

## 3. Baseline inventory — carried forward, still unassigned

§4 promises "a consistent, shared understanding of the entire inventory of software
and technology in use across the department." Phase 3 reviews utilization "against
the intent stated on the original request." Those only connect for technology that
came in through the framework. Anything bought earlier has no originating request, no
owner, and no renewal date on a calendar.

**Say:** "The renewal gate works going forward. It doesn't tell us what we're already
paying for or when those agreements end. Who owns building that baseline, and by
when?"

## 4. Student-Athlete Notice — defined but never required

"Student-Athlete Notice" is defined in §2.4 as a plain-language explanation to
athletes of what data is collected, why, who can access it, retention, and rights.
That definition is the **only** appearance in the framework — no phase step, no role,
and no review level requires one to exist.

**Guard against a false correction:** Form 2 contains the word "notice," but it reads
"Notice period to cancel" — contract cancellation, a different concept. Form 2 does
ask "Consent considerations flagged?", but nothing in the process says who resolves a
flag. So the gap is real; don't let the form's wording talk you out of it.

Related: "Student-Athlete Representatives" is a listed role in §5 providing feedback
on transparency, with no mechanism connecting them to any actual request.

**Why it lands:** §3 states this framework *is* Baylor's implementation of NCAA SSI
guidance, and athlete transparency is central to that guidance. This is the gap most
likely to be flagged when the document reaches OGC and Privacy — better to raise it
yourself now.

## 5. Discontinuation doesn't close the data loop

Phase 3.4 outcomes are "renew, modify or renegotiate, or discontinue." Procurement's
role in §5 requires vendor contracts to address "retention, deletion, portability."
But no phase step triggers return or deletion of athlete data when something is
discontinued. Zero occurrences of exit, termination, or offboarding in the framework.

The contract language exists; nothing invokes it. This is the second item OGC/Privacy
will find.

## 6. Scope — the sentences to point at if pressed

- **§1.2** — "The process below applies to every technology request in the
  department, and the depth of review depends on the request's scope."
- **§2.1** — "The framework is not limited to performance, health, and wellness…
  A request that does not involve any athlete data still goes through the same
  review mechanism."

The phrase "department-wide" no longer appears in §2.1 after the last edit pass; the
idea is fully carried by those two sentences. If someone wants the words back, that's
a one-line change.

## 7. Effective date, in-flight requests, and bypass detection

Three absences with one root — the framework has no occurrences of effective date,
transition, enforcement, violation, or non-compliance:

- No stated start date.
- No handling for requests already underway when this takes effect.
- The Athletics Executive Sponsor "ensures teams, coaches, and staff use approved
  technologies," but nothing states how an off-process purchase is detected or what
  follows. Tie this to item 2 — if it never touches the budget, nothing catches it.

Lowest priority of the seven for a 30-minute initial discussion, but the first
question a skeptical reader asks is "when does this start."

## If it comes up

- **The framework commits other departments' time.** Two control points in fixed
  order, nothing purchased until both clear, ITS represented at a supervisory level
  with authority to commit ITS resources, plus a weekly standing meeting. The
  substantive change worth naming: this moves "who can say no" into a documented
  order. Keep the framing on what the *request* demands, never on staffing.

- **Level declaration is self-proposed.** §6 says the level "is proposed by the
  requester at intake and confirmed at the control points." Confirming it depends on
  knowing what data the technology touches, which at intake is the vendor's claim.
  Fine as designed, but if someone probes the weak point, this is it.

- **Does this become policy?** Currently an Athletics-specific overlay routing into
  existing Baylor governance rather than around it (§2.3). Whether it needs an
  OGC/University policy path is a real question — first thing to drop for time.

- **Urgency gate — already documented.** A request directed by an administrative lead
  enters the expedited lane on receipt and still clears both control points, and
  still carries the review level its data calls for.

## Questions to leave with

2. Who owns the baseline inventory of technology already in use, and by when?
3. Does Student-Athlete Notice become a required step, and at which level?
