# Athletics Technology Inventory — Form Links

Two forms exist. They serve different purposes; keep both.

---

## 1. Quick Inventory — the one to send broadly

Five questions. Name, role, unit, what you use, what you stopped using.
Built for volume and speed — the goal is coverage, not detail.

- **Edit:** https://docs.google.com/forms/d/16gu5e962Iuyun-cyVTg4vxXlW2tLw5MngjcXMALRIxA/edit
- **Preview:** https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSe08JjYGJ3X_fCEvvqjJeyd7U2M9IMog7Wz_E1mkiU6pM-J-Q/viewform

| # | Question | Type | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your name | short text | yes |
| 2 | Your role | short text | yes |
| 3 | Sport or unit | dropdown, 26 options | yes |
| 4 | What technology and software are you currently using? | paragraph | yes |
| 5 | Have you stopped using any technology in the past 12 months? If so, what? | paragraph | no |

data category, funding, contract end, whether it went through the IDEA form, and

## Suggested sequencing

Send the **Quick Inventory** first, department-wide. It answers "what exists," which
is the question Kenny and Jon Allen both raised and the one nothing currently answers.

Use the **Detailed Inventory** as a second pass, aimed only at what the first pass
surfaces as athlete-data systems. Asking seventeen questions per product up front
would suppress response rate; asking five gets the map, and the map tells you where
the detail is worth collecting.
