# Athletics Technology Request — Routing Sheet

**Internal to Athletics. Complete this before submitting an ITS IDEA form or a TCM
contract request.** One page, three questions. It tells you who needs to look at a
request inside Athletics, and it produces a short record that travels with the
request to ITS.

---

## Step 1 — Three questions

**1. Does it collect, store, or display any information about a student-athlete?**
*Names, rosters, video of athletes, performance numbers, survey answers — anything
tied to an individual athlete.*

**2. Does it involve anything about an athlete's body, health, injury, or recovery —
or will athlete information leave Baylor or be handled by an outside company?**
*Sleep, wellness surveys, GPS or load monitoring, force plate, heart rate, injury or
medical notes, rehab or return-to-play status. Also yes if the vendor stores or
processes the data on their own systems.*

**3. Is there a recurring cost, or will more than one team or unit use it?**
☐ Yes  ☐ No  ☐ Not sure

> **"Not sure" counts as Yes.** And data type sets the floor — a free trial, a
> donated license, or a low one-time price never lowers the level.

## Step 2 — Read the level

| If | Level |
|---|---|
| Question 2 is Yes | **Level 3 — Comprehensive Review** |
| Question 2 is No, but Question 1 is Yes | **Level 2 — Enhanced Review** |
| Questions 1 and 2 are No, but Question 3 is Yes | **Level 2 — Enhanced Review** |
| All three are No | **Level 1 — Standard Review** |

## Step 3 — Route it

**Every request starts with the Business Office** — funding, duplication, and whether
Athletics already owns something that does this.

| Level | Route |
|---|---|
| **1 — Standard** | Business Office → ITS IDEA form |
| **2 — Enhanced** | Business Office → Applied Performance + requesting unit → ITS IDEA form |
| **3 — Comprehensive** | Business Office → Applied Performance + Health & Wellness / Sports Medicine sign-off → ITS IDEA form |

## Notes — not part of the sheet

**Why it is built this way.** Micah Lamb's stated objection to the full framework was
that it creates a process parallel to the university's existing workflow. This sheet
does not. It is a front door: it produces better-formed requests entering the existing
IDEA form process, which is what he asked for. Two things he named as the biggest
sources of delay — nobody declaring what data is involved, and nobody knowing whether
the rest of Athletics is aware — are answered by Step 4.

**Why it says "before an IDEA form *or* a TCM request."** Erin Boyette raised that a
request can complete the entire IT review before the Business Office ever sees it,
because the Business Office only appears at the contract stage where TCM requires an
IT project ID. Naming both entry points is what closes that gap.

**The levels are the settled ones.** This sheet does not invent a new scheme — the
three questions just produce Level 1 / 2 / 3 as already defined in §6 of the
framework. The sheet is the simple front end; §6 remains the definition.

**Vendor information, Step 4.** Micah's point: vendors answer security and data
questions readily while they are still selling, and much less readily once they think
the sale is closed. Asking early is worth the thirty seconds.

> ### Settled — entry point
> **Every request starts with the Business Office as the first control point**,
> regardless of level or data type. Confirmed 2026-08-13. No request skips it, and a
> Level 1 request with no athlete data still goes through it before reaching the IDEA
> form.
>
> This matches §8 Phase 1.3 of the framework and what Micah confirmed about how deans
> operate elsewhere on campus — the Business Office pass is a funding and duplication
> check, not an approval decision, so it costs little and catches the case where
> Athletics already owns something that does the job.
