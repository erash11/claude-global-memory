## What validated the framework

Four things in the document were independently named as the fix:

1. **Declaring the data category up front.** Micah's strongest endorsement — this is
   the single biggest source of delay today. "That piece alone will shave weeks off
   the process… that has always been what's taken us the longest." Different people
   call the same data different things; a common language resolves it.
2. **Standardized intake.** "The one thing I saw that I really liked in the proposal
   is standardizing that intake from an athletics perspective." He confirmed ITS
   already does exactly this for other divisions with diverse needs, including a
   priority-ordering step before the request hits the ITS workflow.
3. **Business Office as the first control point.** Kenny described the design and
   Micah confirmed it matches how deans operate — requests go to the business officer
   first for a funding go/no-go that is "not necessarily a decision at that point."
4. **The inventory problem.** Jon Allen: "nobody knows everything that exists."
   80–90% of platforms are known; the remainder surface as surprises years later.
   Kenny closed with "I do think we need to figure out all the systems that we're
   using." This was the meeting's opening theme and its closing one.

## The main risk raised

**Jon Allen's serial-process warning.** Review times have improved substantially, and
he wants to avoid two sequential processes turning a two-week turnaround into eight to
ten weeks. Micah echoed it as "how do we interleave these so it doesn't feel like I'm
doing the same thing two times." Any defense of the framework has to answer this.

Context that makes it concrete: ~1,000 IDEA form requests came through last year,
roughly three a day, arriving in clumps rather than evenly.

## New problems surfaced that the framework does not currently address

- **Multiple entry points are the actual bottleneck.** Micah listed them: email from
  a coach, email from procurement via TCM, IDEA form submission, business office,
  strength and conditioning. Each carries different information. Cody and Kenny put
  it at roughly 80% coming through the right channel and **20% bypassing** — and that
  20% causes disproportionate delay and administrative burden.

- **TCM/IDEA form chicken-and-egg — this one directly contradicts "Business Office
  first."** Erin Boyette explained that a staff member can complete the **entire** IT
  review before the business office ever sees the request, because the business office
  only encounters it at the contract stage, where a TCM submission requires an IT
  project ID. Procurement added that ID requirement to the TCM form without ITS
  knowing. So the university's own mechanics can route around the first control point.

- **Discovery before product selection.** Jon Allen cautioned against people arriving
  at the business office with a product already chosen. Better: "I have this
  challenge" → check whether an existing solution already meets it. The framework has
  no needs-discovery step ahead of intake; it begins with a request for a named product.

- **Cross-domain sign-off is genuinely unresolved.** Micah: "when it crosses those
  domains, is there an expectation that every domain signs off in athletics?" Not
  knowing who to call is a documented cause of delay. This is what §5 Roles is for,
  but the routing is not explicit.

- **Simple vs. complex needs defined parameters.** Lisa: if you create two paths you
  "would have to define what that means… what the parameters are." §6 already does
  this — Level 1/2/3 with stated triggers. Worth pointing at directly, since the room
  asked for something the document already contains.
