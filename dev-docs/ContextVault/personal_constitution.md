# Personal Constitution

*Eric*
*Drafted June 2026*

---

## How to read this document

This is not a list of aspirations. It is an honest account of who I am right now, what I am working toward, and what I am working against. It is meant to be read by me when I need to remember, and by AI systems when they need to understand who they are talking to.

It is written plainly because plain is the only form that has any authority over me. The moment this document starts sounding like a sermon, it stops working.

---

## The foundation

I am a follower of Jesus Christ. He is my Lord and Savior. Everything else in this document sits on top of that fact, and nothing else in this document gets to compete with it.

I believe people are good and also that we are all sinners. Without Christ, the second fact eventually wins, which is why redemption matters. When I walk into a room, my posture is to assume people are good and trying. My floor, underneath that posture, is the knowledge that human nature unredeemed defaults to self-interest. The posture is how I treat people. The floor is what keeps me from being naive when someone shows me who they actually are.

I do not have my faith fully integrated. I am learning to trust God with outcomes, and I am not yet at the place of full acceptance. I want to be honest about that gap, because pretending I have arrived when I have not is its own kind of dishonesty.

---

## What I value

### Service, honestly held

I want to serve. I have discovered that I am at my best when I am in a servant-first posture, whether that is helping a coworker think through a problem, sharing the Gospel with someone who is curious, or pushing the mission of the place I work for.

I also want to be seen. I want my work to be recognized. I want to be considered valuable. These two things sit in tension, and I am not going to pretend the second one does not exist. The work of my life is to keep the first one in front of the second one, even when the second one is louder.

### Action over commentary

Pointing out problems without offering solutions is cheap. Sitting in judgment of leadership when I am not the one carrying the risk is cheap. I do not want to be the man who critiques from the sidelines. I want to be known as someone who identifies problems and brings solutions, who can be counted on to move the mission forward, who takes the action that the situation requires.

When I find myself criticizing without offering anything, that is the signal that I have stepped off the field and started watching the game.

### Honest reckoning over a flattering story

I would rather hear the accurate version of myself than the comfortable one. The accurate version is the only version I can actually work with.

This cuts both directions. I have a tendency to overshoot in the direction of self-condemnation, to reach for harsher language about myself than the facts support, and then have to walk it back when pressed. That is not humility. It is pride wearing humility's clothes. Accuracy is the goal, not self-flagellation and not self-flattery.

### Faithfulness in unseen work

I have made an agreement with myself: for the next twelve months, I am going to do the work whether or not anyone recognizes it. Build the skills, invest in the people, create the frameworks and structures. If the value gets seen, that is a gift. If it does not, I move my family somewhere else or into another industry. Either way, the work itself is the point, not the recognition of it.

External validation is not what I am chasing anymore. I want to mean that. I am still learning what it feels like to mean it.

## What I believe

I believe God is in control. I believe He has a plan for my life that includes the parts I would not have chosen. I believe my job is to do what is in front of me with integrity and to trust Him with what is not in front of me.

I believe I cannot strategize my way into the life I want. I spent years operating as if I could, moving pieces around, working harder, being smarter, saying the right thing at the right time. It produced anxiety and disappointment, not life. Surrender produces life. I am still learning to surrender.

I believe prayer is not a backup plan. It is the actual plan. When fear or resentment shows up in me, the answer is not to manage them better. The answer is to pray and to ask God to soften the parts of my heart that are still hard.

I believe success measured in money, prestige, title, or recognition is a counterfeit. I do not want to be the man whose epitaph is a list of accomplishments and a quiet absence of the people who knew him best.

---

## What I am working against

### Resentment is my tell

When I find myself resentful, that is the clearest signal that I have not yet surrendered something I claim to have surrendered. Resentment is not the sin. It is the diagnostic. When it shows up, the work is to bring it to God, not to suppress it and not to feed it.

### Self-righteousness toward leadership

I have spent too long being critical of leadership in my head. It is comfortable because it costs nothing. There is no risk in saying I would do it better when I am not the one doing it. That is chickenshit, to use my own word for it. If I have something to offer, I should offer it through action, not commentary.

### The fear underneath the anger

The deepest trigger for the worst version of me is fear about my work. Fear that I am not seen as valuable. Fear that I will be let go. Fear that I am out here figuring it out alone every day and getting it wrong. That fear, more than tiredness or frustration, is what leaks out onto Kristina and the kids when I am short with them or impatient or unkind.

The principle: **when I feel the fear about work, that is the moment to pause and pray, because that is the moment most likely to make me hurt the people I love.**

### The man I am trying not to become

He is the man who thinks he has all the answers. He rides the highs and lows of life because he has tied his sense of self to outcomes he cannot control. He idolizes recognition. He talks about Jesus but does not live like Jesus is at the center of anything. People who knew him would describe him by his job, his accomplishments, the money he made. His wife and children would describe him as someone who was around but not really there.

I do not want to be that man. The way I avoid becoming him is not by trying harder to be a good man. It is by surrendering daily to the only One who can actually change me.

## The people who matter most

My wife Kristina, my son Ames (5), my daughter Saylor (4), and my youngest Rowan (10 months). They are the real arena of my life.

Work is where my ego shows up loudest, which is why it consumes so much of my attention. Home is where my character is actually being built or wasted. If I nail my career and fail with my family, I have lost. If my work is never recognized and Kristina and the kids know who I actually was, I have won.

Twenty years from now, I want my children to be able to say:

- He loved Jesus, and we could tell.
- He loved their mom, and we could tell.
- He worked hard for us and he told us why.
- He was not the greatest dad ever, but he was consistent, he tried, and he was honest about his failures.
- He wanted us to love our neighbors, love Jesus, and want the best for the world, and the way he lived made that easier to believe.

I fail them most often by being selfish, by not putting them before my own interests, by bringing my work fear home as impatience or unkindness, and by not consistently showing them how much I love them. These are the failures I am most committed to addressing, because these are the ones that actually matter.

## How I want to be in the world

I treat people with the assumption that they are good and trying. I do not assume the worst.

I do not pretend to have it figured out. I am willing to be the person in the room who says "I don't know" or "I was wrong."

I take action when I see a problem. If I am going to name a problem, I bring a solution with me, or I name it as a question rather than a complaint.

I serve first. When I notice I am performing service in order to be seen, I name it to myself, and I keep serving anyway, because the alternative is to stop serving, which is worse.

I am honest about my faith, including the parts that are still under construction. I would rather be a Christian who is openly still learning than a Christian who is privately performing.

I do not curse at or about people. I do not speak with contempt about groups of people I do not know. When I catch myself doing it, I stop, and if it was out loud, I apologize.

I work hard, but I do not work as if my work is the source of my worth. My worth is fixed by Christ. My work is an offering.

---

## What I want this document to do

I want to be able to read this on a hard Tuesday and have it tell me the truth. Not flatter me. Not condemn me. Tell me the truth about who I am, what I am working toward, and what I am up against.

I want any AI system reading this to understand that I am not asking to be told I am doing well. I want to be challenged when I am vague, pushed when I am inconsistent, and asked harder questions when my answers sound rehearsed. I would rather be sharpened than soothed.

I want to be the kind of man whose private life and public life are the same life. I am not there yet. This document is part of how I get there.
