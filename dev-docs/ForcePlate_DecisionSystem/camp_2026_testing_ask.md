# Testing cadence: Fall Camp 2026 through the season

**The ask:** move the IMTP from weekly to **block boundaries (4× per season)**,
and use the freed Monday slots for a **second CMJ**.

Net effect: CMJ on Monday and Thursday most weeks. IMTP still tested, but at
the rate it actually changes.

Nothing new is purchased. No added athlete time — this is a swap, not an
addition.

---

## Why we're asking

Currently an athlete does **either** IMTP **or** CMJ on a given day, never
both. Across 2023–25 there are 2,029 athlete-days of IMTP and exactly **one**
of them also has a CMJ. Monday is IMTP day, Thursday is CMJ day.

That means we only ever see a player's jump **once** in a week — at the end of
it. A single Thursday test tells us where a player *is*. It cannot tell us
what the week *did to him*, because there's no Monday starting point to
compare against.

| In-season athlete-weeks, 2023–25 | Count |
|---|---|
| Thursday CMJ only | 2,375 |
| Monday CMJ only | 314 |
| **Both (usable pair)** | **687 — 20% of weeks** |

## Why the IMTP is the slot to move

**This is not a claim that the IMTP is a poor test.** It is a claim that we
are measuring it about ten times faster than it changes.

We looked at every pair of IMTP tests on the same athlete and asked how much
the number moves as the gap between tests grows. If a quality is genuinely
developing, the spread of change should widen over months. If it is only
noise, the spread stays flat.

**IMTP — Peak Vertical Force / BM:**

| Gap between tests | Spread of change |
|---|---|
| Within 10 days | 13.2% |
| 25–50 days | 16.5% |
| 100–200 days | 17.3% |

Separating those: the **week-to-week noise is 13.2%**, and the **real strength
change accumulated across 3–6 months is about 11%**.

Read that again, because it is the whole argument: **half a season of genuine
strength change is smaller than one week of measurement noise.** A weekly IMTP
number is essentially all noise. Four well-spaced tests carry the same
information as thirteen, because the thing being measured only moves on a
months-long timescale — which is exactly what you'd expect from maximal
strength, and the data agrees with the physiology.

By comparison, the CMJ carries **half the noise** (6.6% within 10 days for
Peak Power / BM) and is the measure that responds inside a week.

## What we'd keep

**IMTP 4× per season** at points where a real change could have accumulated:

- Start of camp
- End of camp / start of season
- Mid-season
- End of season

That preserves every meaningful strength comparison — start vs end of camp,
in-season retention, year-over-year development — because those are all
month-plus intervals. We lose nothing except measurements that were noise.

That frees roughly **9 Mondays per season** for the second CMJ.

## One thing we should fix regardless of this decision

The **RFD metrics are not usable at any cadence.** RFD-100ms has 36.6%
week-to-week noise; RFD-200ms has 23.8%. At that level, a change would have to
be enormous before it meant anything, and we should not be making decisions
off either number. Peak Vertical Force (8.8%) is the IMTP number worth
keeping.

## The one question we asked first

Before proposing any of this we asked whether the Monday IMTP is serving as a
**training or priming stimulus** rather than only as a measurement — a maximal
pull to start the week. If it were, everything above would be beside the point,
because that is a strength-and-conditioning judgment rather than a data one.

**It is not.** The Monday IMTP is there to measure, not to prime. That is what
makes the swap a clean one: removing nine of those Mondays removes measurement
we've shown carries no information, and removes no training stimulus.

Our analysis on its own speaks only to the *measurement* value of a weekly
IMTP, which is close to zero. The training question was settled separately, by
the staff who program the week.

## What the change buys on the CMJ side

With the current ~8 usable paired weeks per athlete, our ability to detect a
real effect of the training week is about **4%** — indistinguishable from
guessing. The swap takes us to roughly **19 paired weeks per athlete**, where
that becomes about **98%**.

It would let us answer:

- Which players hold up under a heavy week and which don't
- Whether a specific camp block is costing more than it's buying
- Whether an individual's dip this week is meaningful or just noise

## Camp specifically

Camp force-plate testing has fallen off:

| Camp (August) | Test days | Tests per athlete |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 7 | 4 |
| 2024 | **1** | 1 |
| 2025 | **2** | 1 |

Camp is the hardest three weeks of the year and we currently have one snapshot
per player. The 2023 pattern worked; we should return to it.

## One honest note

We are not promising a big finding. Our current best read is that a normal
Baylor practice week does **not** measurably fatigue these athletes — the mean
Monday-to-Thursday change is under 2% on every metric, and roughly half of
players go up rather than down.

That may be real credit to how the week is built. But right now we cannot
distinguish "our week is well designed" from "we can't see what our week is
doing." This change is what separates those two answers.

## A caution on how we read any of these numbers

At the individual level, none of our force-plate metrics can currently resolve
a one-week change small enough to be worth acting on. **A single player's
single-week move is mostly noise, on CMJ and IMTP alike.** These numbers are
trustworthy as **trends across several tests** — which is another argument for
testing more regularly, not less.
